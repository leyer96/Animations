The Schrödinger equation, in motion

In quantum mechanics, a particle isn't described by a position and a velocity but by a wave function Ψ(r, t). One equation tells you how it changes in time.

The equation

For a single particle of mass \(m\) in a potential \(V(\mathbf r)\), the time-dependent Schrödinger equation reads

$$ i\hbar\,\frac{\partial \Psi(\mathbf r,t)}{\partial t} \;=\; -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\nabla^2 \Psi(\mathbf r,t) \;+\; V(\mathbf r)\,\Psi(\mathbf r,t), $$

where in two dimensions the Laplacian is \(\nabla^2 = \partial^2/\partial x^2 + \partial^2/\partial y^2\) (add \(\partial^2/\partial z^2\) in three).

  • The left side is the rate of change of Ψ in time, multiplied by \(i\hbar\).
  • The Laplacian term is the kinetic energy. It's large where Ψ curves sharply, which is why tightly localized waves spread out.
  • The potential term bends, reflects and traps the wave, the way forces act on a classical particle.

Ψ itself is complex-valued and not directly observable. What you can measure is the probability density \(|\Psi(\mathbf r,t)|^2\): the chance of finding the particle near \(\mathbf r\) at time \(t\). Because the particle has to be somewhere, the wave function is normalized,

$$ \int |\Psi(\mathbf r,t)|^2 \, d^2 r = 1, $$

and the Schrödinger equation keeps this integral constant at all times.

A Gaussian wave packet

A free particle with a fairly well-defined position and momentum is described by a Gaussian wave packet, centered at \(\mathbf r_0\) with width \(\sigma\) and mean wave vector \(\mathbf k_0\):

$$ \Psi(\mathbf r,0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\!\left(-\frac{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_0|^2}{4\sigma^2}\right) e^{\,i\,\mathbf k_0\cdot \mathbf r}. $$

The Gaussian envelope localizes the particle, and the plane-wave factor gives it momentum \(\mathbf p_0 = \hbar \mathbf k_0\). Left alone, the packet moves at the group velocity \(\mathbf v_g = \hbar \mathbf k_0/m\) and spreads in every direction: its width grows as \(\sigma(t) = \sigma\sqrt{1 + \left(\hbar t / 2m\sigma^2\right)^2}\).
